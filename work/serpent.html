Case study — Snake identification & venom safety

Serpent

After a snake sighting or bite, a confident wrong answer can cost a life. So the app is allowed to say “Unknown”.

Role

UI/UX design, front-end, AI integration

Tools

Figma, Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS

The problem

People who see a snake — or have just been bitten — often guess wrong about whether it’s dangerous. An app that guesses confidently can make that worse.

What made it hard

  • The user may be panicking, injured, or holding a phone in one hand
  • Photo quality is often poor, so the AI can be unsure
  • It has to work for English, Thai and Burmese speakers
  • Species names must stay searchable for doctors

Three decisions

01

Say “Unknown” instead of guessing

One photo is compared against 4,245 species. If the match isn’t strong enough, the result says Unknown and points to safety advice — never false reassurance.

02

Emergency first, always one tap away

A red “I’ve been bitten” button sits in the header on every page and opens a calm, step-by-step emergency flow.

03

Three languages, untranslated species names

The interface works in English, Thai and Burmese, but scientific and common species names stay as they are, so they match what hospitals and searches use.

What I learned

Designing for stress means removing choices, not adding them. Small details still matter — custom dropdowns for the species filters, and a viewfinder hover on the main button that hints at what it does — but the biggest design decision was what the app refuses to say.
